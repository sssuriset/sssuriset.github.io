Flight software simulator

A C++ simulator for the software side of a small spacecraft. It holds subsystem state, a periodic task scheduler, timestamped command processing, fault detection with protective mode entry, and CSV telemetry logging.

One loop iteration is one second of spacecraft time across a thirty second scenario with eclipse entry at t = 15 s. Malformed command lines are reported and skipped rather than aborting the run, so one bad uplink costs one command instead of the whole pass.

Low Earth orbit · C and Python

Attitude control and state estimation

An attitude control testbed split the way flight software is actually structured. C flight software holds the PD controller and an RTOS-style scheduler running sensor, control, telemetry, and health tasks at 100, 50, 10, and 1 Hz. A Python plant carries the dynamics with sensor noise, gyro random-walk bias, disturbance torque, and reaction wheel saturation. A Python ground segment ingests the logged telemetry into SQLite and flags threshold anomalies.

A per-axis Kalman filter with a gyro bias state cuts steady-state pointing error RMS about 8× and torque activity about 3× against raw sensors. Process noise is derived from the modeled disturbance torque with no free tuning, so the filter is not fit to the answer it is meant to find. A 20 seed Monte Carlo study holds per-axis mean ANEES at 3.19 to 3.37 inside chi-square 95% bounds and mean ANIS at 1.98 to 2.02 against a target of 2.

The same testbed extends to proximity operations. Clohessy-Wiltshire relative dynamics in the Hill frame, with the closed-form state transition matrix checked against numerical propagation over two orbits, drive a 500 m -> 50 m V-bar approach. Two-impulse transfer costs 0.68 m/s and closed-loop glideslope guidance costs 2.07 m/s, which prices the robustness. An EKF estimating relative state from 1 Hz range, azimuth, and elevation reaches 10.5 cm steady-state RMSE against 0.5 m range noise. A 30 m keep-out sphere, closing-speed corridor, and latched abort are evaluated onboard on the estimated state, and a mis-commanded approach aborts at t = 1474 s with 39.9 m minimum true range.

0.40 rad peak transient to 3.1 × 10−4 rad final pointing error Kp = 0.8, Kd = 2.0, torque limit 0.2 N·m 10.5 cm relative-nav RMSE, abort at 39.9 m true range

Lunar image quality metrics

A batch quality inspection workflow for lunar surface imagery. Each image produces a percentile-normalized version, a contrast stretch, a Canny edge map, a residual noise image, and a set of tiles.

Seventeen image-quality metrics are written to CSV so images in a batch can be compared numerically instead of by eye. Normalization rescales between the 1st and 99th percentile rather than absolute min and max, since a few extreme pixels can flatten the useful brightness range of a whole image.

Sharpness from the variance of the Laplacian Batch ranking, not calibrated surface measurement

Perihelion, inside 1 AU · Python

Integrator error against timestep

Three numerical integration methods tested on a two-body Earth orbit. Explicit Euler, semi-implicit Euler, and velocity Verlet. Acceleration comes from a = −μr / |r|³, with Earth as a fixed central mass and the satellite as a point mass.

Each method runs across timesteps from 1 s to 300 s over seven days, measuring energy and angular momentum conservation, period estimate, and final position error. At dt = 300 s explicit Euler gains roughly 100% in specific energy and leaves the orbit entirely, semi-implicit Euler oscillates inside a bounded band with no secular drift, and velocity Verlet stays flat. Drag, thrust, third-body gravity, and oblateness are omitted deliberately, so what the study measures is integration error rather than perturbation.

Timesteps 1 s to 300 s, seven-day span NumPy and Matplotlib

PFSS source-surface height sensitivity

How sensitive are coronal-to-1 AU magnetic mapping predictions to the choice of source-surface height? Potential-field source-surface models were built from HMI synoptic magnetograms at 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0 solar radii, ballistically mapped to 1 AU, and tested against OMNI hourly solar wind observations across five Carrington rotations.

The equatorial open-flux proxy correlates with near-Earth field strength at Spearman r near 0.30 over the pooled sample, and the correlation survives a phase-shift null test. Height sensitivity is modest but consistent. 2.5 solar radii is strongest in the pooled sample and in four of five leave-one-rotation-out subsets.

First author, poster at AAS SPD 57, Baltimore, August 2026 180 pooled points, null test p = 0.002 to 0.007 CRs 2281, 2283, 2284, 2286, 2290

Period search against ground truth

Period searches on stellar light curves, run as two experiments against one shared search module. The first measures a variability period in a real Kepler light curve. The second validates the search on a synthetic curve with a known injected transit, so recovery accuracy can be checked against ground truth.

The pair shows why method choice matters. Lomb-Scargle finds the Kepler star's smooth 19.4005 day variability cleanly. On a box-shaped transit signal it locks onto a 1.24 day alias, while Box Least Squares recovers the injected 3.72 day period to within 1.4 minutes.

KIC 757450, Q3 long cadence, 4134 of 4370 points kept Sine-fit period 19.4013 d, residual RMS 0.0033

~1019 m · GLSL ES 3.00

Schwarzschild ray tracer

A real-time general-relativistic ray tracer in a single HTML file. Every pixel integrates a null geodesic backward from the camera through the Schwarzschild metric in a fragment shader. Geodesics are planar, so integration runs in each ray's own orbital plane using the Binet form, d²u/dφ² = 3u² − u, which needs two state variables per ray instead of eight.

The disk is a thin Keplerian annulus from the ISCO out to 20M, shaded with the exact circular-orbit redshift and g⁴ bolometric beaming. A toggle sets g = 1 to show the same disk without relativistic shading, which separates the physics from the rendering.

Shadow radius 41.5 px measured, 41.0 px predicted Deflection rel. err 8.8 × 10−5 at b = 100M

~1022 m · Python

Hubble constant on a planted sample

A Hubble constant estimation study on a controlled synthetic galaxy sample. The generator plants the truth at H₀ = 67.5 km/s/Mpc with distance and redshift uncertainty, peculiar velocity scatter, and one injected outlier, so every fitting choice is judged against a known answer.

A weighted fit through the origin on the full sample returns 68.39. Removing the planted outlier at 95 Mpc with a 2σ residual cut moves it to 67.60. One bad galaxy shifts the answer by 0.8 km/s/Mpc, which is the point of the exercise.

Bootstrap 95% interval 66.76 to 71.19 Residual RMS 359 to 208 km/s after the cut

~4 × 1026 m · Python

CMB acoustic peak extraction

Feature extraction on the Planck cosmic microwave background TT power spectrum. The script smooths the spectrum with a Savitzky-Golay filter, locates the first three acoustic peaks by prominence and spacing, converts each peak location to an angular scale through θ = 180 / ℓ, and writes the measurements to CSV with figures.

Peak detection runs on the smoothed spectrum with a prominence threshold of 0.20 standard deviations and a minimum separation of 120 in ℓ. The scope is peak measurement and derived scales, not cosmological parameter fitting.

Peaks at ℓ = 224, 514, 822 First peak angular scale 0.80° Mean peak spacing Δℓ = 299
